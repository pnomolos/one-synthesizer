MPE and external control for the Moog One

Working notes from an interoperability project: getting an MPE controller to play a Moog One with per-note pitch, pressure and timbre, and driving its parameter tree from a computer.

The instrument is internally a small network. An ARM Linux module runs the UI; an XMOS microcontroller (the voice controller) receives MIDI, allocates voices and talks to the analog voice cards. The three speak OSC to each other over UDP and an internal link. Almost everything here follows from mapping that traffic.

  • Signal flow: how a note gets from a key or a MIDI port to a voice card, where the built-in keyboard and an external controller take different paths, and how per-note MPE fits in.
  • Protocol: ports, the MIDI-in-OSC encoding, the binary parameter record format, and reply semantics.
  • Editor: a desktop editor and librarian for the parameter surface, in progress.

What is published here

Documentation of observed behaviour and interfaces, plus diagrams. Original source code will follow per track as it is cleaned up.

Not published, and not going to be: Moog firmware, in original or modified form, or disassembly and string dumps of it. Anything that eventually ships for the per-note MPE track will be a patch that operates on an image you extract from your own instrument.

Risk. Everything here that sends OSC to the voice controller or modifies it can leave the instrument unresponsive. The controller has no read verb on its parameter port (every argument is a write), and one stray message to a mode address hung it hard enough to need a physical power cycle. Treat the protocol notes as a map, not an invitation.

Tested configuration

One instrument: system firmware 1.5.0, voice controller 2.1.2, voice cards 2.1.0. Nothing here is known to hold on other versions.
